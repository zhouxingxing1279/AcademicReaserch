Single-Beacon Localization for Mobile Robot: A Set
Membership Filtering Approach
Xujie Qin , Yirui Cong , Member, IEEE, Jun Lai , Jinyi Yang , and Xiangke Wang , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—In this letter, we study the localization problem of
a mobile robot with range measurement from a single beacon.
Previous filtering-based studies usually required accurate statistics
of noises to be theoretically sound and reliable, which is difficult to
obtain in practical systems. To solve this problem, we propose an ac-
curate and efficient localization method based on a set-membership
filtering framework with constrained zonotopes. This method in-
cludes three novel steps. First, we design a convex optimization
relaxation method to handle the non-convexity caused by the single-
beacon range measurement. Then, a halfspace-intersection refine-
ment is proposed which improves the estimation accuracy. Finally,
we provide a sliding-window recursive method that simultaneously
guarantees the accuracy and the efficiency. Simulations and field
experiments corroborate the effectiveness of our proposed method.
Index Terms—Single-beacon localization, range measurement,
set-membership filtering, constrained zonotope.
I. INTRODUCTION
RANGE-BASED localization for mobile robot systems
has attracted significant research interest in recent
years [1], [2], [3]. Different from the Global Positioning Sys-
tem (GPS)/inertial navigation [4] and the Vision-Based local-
ization [5], range-based localization usually relies on wireless
communications; thus, it can not only enable robotic localization
in a wider range of environments but also improve localization
accuracy through information fusion. Most of the current range-
basedlocalizationmethodsthroughmulti-beacon[6],requirethe
installation of complex beacon networks in advance. However, if
the beacon network can only be deployed temporarily while the
robot is performing tasks, the network may only have one beacon
available, which renders the existing methods inapplicable.
Single-beacon localization methods in robot systems have
been studied recently in [7], [8], [9]. The main focus of the
research is to fuse the single-beacon range measurement and the
existing localization system to improve overall performance. For
example, Yang et al. [7] improved the accuracy of the Inertial
Measurement Unit (IMU) data via single-beacon ranging mea-
surement; a sensor fusion method for Ultra-wideband (UWB)

Note that sensor noise is a main factor affecting localization
performance. In the literature, to attenuate the negative effects
of the noise, the research of single-beacon localization relies on
stochastic filters such as the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) [10]
or Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) [11]. Stochastic filters per-
form well when the noise conforms to known distribution [12].
However, in real robot systems, the statistical characteristics of
noise are assumed by approximation (e.g., Gaussian distribu-
tion); if this approximation significantly deviates from the real
distribution, the filtering results are inaccurate [13].
Different from the stochastic filters, the Set-Membership
Filterings (SMFs) [14], [15] only require the range of noises
and without any statistical information, and the unknown-but-
bounded (UBB) model is used to describe the noise. The SMF
aims to estimate compact sets containing the true state of the
mobile robot system. In recent years, many SMF state esti-
mation methods with different set representation tools have
been proposed. For example, Wang et al. [16] proposed an
ellipsoidal SMF method with a new estimator, which improved
the accuracy of estimation through effective parameters opti-
mization. Tang et al. [17] provided an SMF method based on
zonotopes, which minimized zonotopes via observer parameter
optimization. Rego et al. [18] designed an SMF method based on
constrained zonotopes, which recursively updated constrained
zonotopes through mean value and first-order taylor extension.
Based on the theoretical breakthroughs, the SMF method has
drawn increasing attention to solving the localization problem
of mobile robots. A lane boundary augmented set-membership
global navigation satellite systems positioning was presented
in [19], which computed the absolute robotic position of geo-
metric constraints by bounded noises of the sensor. A global
set-membership filtering approach was proposed in [20], which
obtained accurate localization results of automatic guided vehi-
cles based on a distributed localization network. Nevertheless,
research on the SMF localization method for the range measure-
ment is currently limited. In [21], a constrain-convex-generator
method was provided to deal with the non-convexity of the
2D range measurements by cutting the measurement annulus.
Using a similar idea, [22] designed a cooperative SMF for
distributed localization. Even though the above method can be
appliedtothesingle-beaconlocalizationproblem,unfortunately,
it faces the issue of balancing the computational complexity and
the accuracy. To the best of our knowledge, SMF localization
problem for the single-beacon range measurement is still open.
In this letter, we propose an accurate and efficient method
based on constrained zonotopes for the single-beacon localiza-
tion problem with UBB noises. The main contributions are as

V. CONCLUSION
In this letter, we have investigated an accurate and efficient
set-membership filtering method based constrained zonotopes
for the single-beacon localization problem. To handle the chal-
lenge of requiring accurate statistics of noises for previous
filtering-based studies, we have proposed a new solution for
single-beacon localization by set-membership filtering with
UBB noises. We have designed a convex optimization to handle
the non-convexity caused by the single-beacon range measure-
ment and a halfspace-intersection refinement to improve the
accuracy of localization. At the same time, a sliding-window
recursive method has been proposed to ensure accuracy and
efficiency. Simulation and field experiment have proved the
effectiveness and the advantages of the SMF-based localization
method. For future work, we will explore effective localization
methods for specific classes of nonlinear models (e.g., the uni-
cycle model).
