A Fast and Accurate Linear Constrained Zonotopic
Set-membership Filter
Xujie Qin, Yirui Cong, Member, IEEE, Xiangke Wang, Senior Member, IEEE, and
Tengfei Liu, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—This article proposes a fast and accurate linear
constrained zonotopic set-membership filter (CZ-SMF), where
the interval hulls of the estimates are computed online in real
time. Unlike existing methods solving interval hulls independently
and inefficiently at different time steps, we effectively leverage
the temporal relations inherent in the recursive filtering pro-
cess. More specifically, we establish two fundamental properties
of linear CZ-SMFs: (i) structural invariance, which provides
invariant parameters in constrained zonotopic estimates over
time, and (ii) parametric similarity that guarantees the uniform
boundedness of the time-varying optimization parameters for
consecutive time steps. Inspired by the fundamental properties,
we develop the structural-parametric information-based SMF
(SPIN-SMF), which offers two important advantages. First, our
designed optimization algorithm only involves linear operations
by exploiting the structural invariance to precompute matrix
inversions. Second, utilizing the parametric similarity, we propose
a warm-start strategy to efficiently initialize the optimization pro-
cess. Baseline and Monte Carlo simulations show that the SPIN-
SMF reduces computation time by 98.72%-99.98% compared to
the classical SMFs while maintaining high accuracy.
Index Terms—Set-membership filter, constrained zonotope,
fast and accurate algorithm.
I. INTRODUCTION
Constrained zonotopic set-membership filter (CZ-SMF)1
has emerged as a key framework for robust state estimation in
dynamical systems with bounded noises and limited statistical
properties [1]–[3]. With high estimation accuracy [4], the CZ-
SMF has demonstrated its great potential in applications such
as localization [5] and fault diagnosis [6].
Despite its accuracy advantages, the CZ-SMF faces signif-
icant computational bottlenecks in practice. Specifically, the
real-time applicability of the CZ-SMF depends on solving
the interval hulls for the constrained zonotopic estimates effi-
ciently and accurately [4]; however, efficiently solving interval
hulls is challenging, even for linear systems, as the complexity
of constrained zonotopes grows unboundedly due to recursive
prediction-update operations [1].

To deal with the growth of complexity while maintaining
guaranteed estimates, current research has developed various
outer-approximation order reduction techniques. These tech-
niques approximate high-complexity constrained zonotopes
with lower-complexity ones that tightly contain them. Geomet-
rically, constrained zonotopes are described by generators (the
same as traditional zonotopes [7]) and linear constraints. Based
on the geometric properties of constrained zonotopes, [1] pro-
posed order reduction methods for the constraints (associated
with a rescaling strategy) and generators, respectively. Note
that for the generator reduction, a lift-then-reduce strategy [1]
can be employed, allowing the order reduction techniques for
traditional zonotopes to be applied directly: for example, the
first systematic method for generator reduction [7], seminal
generator sorting strategies [8] and [9], and a principal com-
ponent analysis (PCA)-based reduction method [10]. Different
from the above geometry-based methods, an Observation-
Information Tower-inspired method (OIT-CZ SMF) [11] was
developed recently, by only utilizing the properties of dynami-
cal systems to achieve efficient order reduction. These methods
effectively address the complexity-growth issue and have been
widely used [2], [3], [6].
While existing linear CZ-SMFs achieved a balance between
estimation accuracy and computational efficiency for solving
interval hulls at individual time steps, their relations caused
by the recursive filtering process were totally and implicitly
overlooked. This leads to low efficiency when sequentially
solving interval hulls: 1) existing methods did not utilize
(or even disrupt2
) common/invariant structures across the
estimated sets in different time steps, which could have
significantly accelerated the computations; 2) solving interval
hulls independently ignores the inherent parametric similarity
between the optimization problems at consecutive time steps,
where the similarity can help remove the redundancy in com-
putations. Thus, it is necessary to seek fundamental properties
of sequentially solving interval hulls for linear CZ-SMFs and
develop fast and accurate algorithms accordingly.
In this work, we propose a fast and accurate CZ-SMF for
linear time-invariant systems. The key novelty over existing
methods (e.g., [1], [7]–[11]) is to exploit the temporal relation
of recursive interval hull computations. It substantially reduces
online computational complexity while preserving high accu-
racy. Our contributions are as follows:

VI. CONCLUSION
In this article, we have proposed a fast and accurate linear
CZ-SMF – the SPIN-SMF, inspired by our established two
fundamental properties: structural invariance and parametric
similarity. For the structural invariance, it provides invariant
parameters in constrained zonotopic estimates over time; for
the parametric similarity, it guarantees the uniform bound-
edness of the other parameters (which are time-varying) in
consecutive time steps. By leveraging these properties, the
SPIN-SMF achieves significant computational efficiency im-
provements. Specifically, structural invariance enables precom-
putation of matrix inversions such that our algorithm only
involves linear operations; Parametric similarity supports an
efficient warm-start strategy by reusing information from the
previous time step to initialize our algorithm. For future work,
we will explore extending the SPIN-SMF to linear time-
varying systems and nonlinear systems.
