# Run190 measurement-conditioned extreme family design

## Intent

Run189 showed that physical containment alone produces one dominating information set. This run asks the next smaller question: can the actual bounded position measurement, without arbitrary offset caps, produce at least two incomparable maximal information sets in one source mode?

## Goals

- Start from the certified vertical physical box and the certified position-noise halfwidth.
- Construct the exact family of posteriors generated by every nonempty measurement strip.
- Express the maximal posterior family in the six-facet direct-offset coordinates.
- Distinguish the continuum of true extreme sets from the two vertices of its one-dimensional offset parameter polytope.
- Produce exact rational and hash-bound evidence.

## Non-goals

- No 15-mode/17-edge propagation, control synthesis, terminal set, or recursive-feasibility claim.
- No claim that two parameter vertices exhaust the true extreme-set boundary.
- No claim that measurement closure removes the Run184 velocity-radius obstruction.
- No six-state nonlinear quadrotor guarantee or novelty claim.

## Approaches considered

1. **Arbitrary offset caps.** Rejected: Run189 and the literature gate show that such caps have no information-theoretic justification.
2. **Two isolated translated posterior boxes.** Rejected: this is not closed under the continuous measurement-center domain and would hide the intermediate maximal sets.
3. **Recommended: the full continuous measurement-generated family.** Use every nonempty position strip. Interior strips yield equal-width translated rectangles; boundary strips are subsets of the endpoint rectangles. The downward closure is an information ensemble, every full-width translated rectangle is maximal, and the offset parameters form an affine segment with two vertices.

## Exact construction

Let `X=[-P,P] x [-V,V]`, with `P=1`, `V=11/4`, and let `a=1/50`. For measurement center `eta`, define `S(eta)={|p-eta|<=a}`. Nonempty posteriors occur exactly for `eta in [-P-a,P+a]`.

For `c in C=[-P+a,P-a]`, define

`R(c)=[c-a,c+a] x [-V,V]`.

The ensemble is the collection of compact subsets of at least one `R(c)`. For any admissible `eta`, `X intersect S(eta)` is contained in `R(clip(eta,C))`; it equals `R(eta)` for `eta in C`. Equal-width rectangles satisfy `R(c1) subseteq R(c2)` iff `c1=c2`, so the true extreme boundary is the continuum `{R(c): c in C}`.

In facet order `(+p,+v,-q,-p,-v,+q)`, `q=p-v/10`, the tight offsets are

`y(c)=(c+a, V, -c+a+V/10, -c+a, V, c+a+V/10)`.

This is an affine segment. Its two parameter vertices are `y(-P+a)` and `y(P-a)`. They are incomparable extreme sets and their convex hull equals the physical box, but they are only finite representatives, not the whole true extreme boundary.

## Verification

Tests must independently check strip/posterior endpoint formulas, six-direction supports, configuration-cone membership, pairwise inclusion, endpoint parameter vertices, convex-hull coverage, and explicit limitations. The archived JSON must be reproducible and hash-bound to code, tests, adapter, six-facet template, configuration, and this design.

## Stop condition

If the exact family is not measurement closed, if any distinct maximal members contain one another, or if the six-offset map is not affine/configured, stop with a counterexample. Passing this gate only permits the next run to study a nondegenerate correlated source family or one-step dynamics; it does not permit the full mode graph yet.
